How to use any of these dashboards

Every dashboard follows the same layout. The top-left tile is a scorecard of headline numbers. Next to it is a commentary panel — a sentence or two written by the workbook itself from the current numbers, so it rewrites when you change anything. The large chart is the main trend or comparison. The right-hand column holds the controls: colour legends first, then parameters (typed inputs or sliders that feed calculations) and filters (checkbox lists that narrow the data).

Filters vs. parameters. A filter removes rows — untick "LTL" and the dashboard recalculates with LTL lanes gone. A parameter is a number you type that formulas use — change "Target Cost per Unit" and the variance, status and commentary all recompute, but no data is removed.

Other things worth knowing: hover any mark for a tooltip; click a legend entry to highlight it (click again to clear); the tabs across the top open each worksheet on its own; the toolbar at the bottom has undo, reset (revert to the published state), download and share. A typed parameter takes effect when you press Enter or click away.

1. Freight Spend WBR — Transportation Scorecard

Question it answers: Is the transportation network hitting its cost, service and settlement targets this week, and what should leadership do about it?

Data: 24 lanes across 3 regions, 4 modes and 6 carriers, 78 weeks of units, spend, on-time %, damage %, invoice accuracy, days to settle and fuel share.

Link: FreightSpendWBR-TransportationScorecard/FreightSpendWBR

Full dashboard. Week 78 is the selected week; targets are the defaults.

Visuals

KPI Scorecard

Nine numbers for the selected week: Cost per Unit (spend ÷ units, the headline metric), CPU Var to Target % (how far cost per unit is above or below your target), CPU What-If (cost per unit after the four what-if sliders are applied), Spend ($M), Units (k), On-Time % and Damage Rate % (both weighted by units), Days to Settle (weighted by spend) and Network Health Score (0–100 composite, see below).

Cost per Unit and On-Time are the two numbers a transportation controller looks at first.

Commentary

Written automatically from the numbers: cost per unit vs. your target, the variance, the RAG status ("On track / Watch / Off track"), the health score, and what cost per unit would be with the current what-if sliders. Move any slider or change a target and this sentence rewrites itself.

CPU Trend vs Target

Cost per unit for the 26 weeks up to the selected week. The horizontal grey line is the Target Cost per Unit parameter, so the gap between line and target is the story. Weeks above the line are over target.

Spend by Mode

Total spend in the selected week split by mode (Air, Intermodal, LTL, Truckload). Colours match the Mode legend at the top right. Use it to see which mode is carrying the budget.

Lane Watchlist

One row per lane in the selected week: CPU Var % (variance to target for that lane), Cost per Unit, Lane Opportunity $ (how many dollars the lane is above or below target — variance × units, so the biggest positive numbers are the biggest savings opportunities) and Units (k). Lanes with fewer units than the Min Lane Units parameter are hidden so small lanes don't distract.

2. Reader Growth Pulse — Subscription Analytics

Question it answers: Where are new subscribers coming from, are they staying, and is the onboarding experiment worth shipping?

Data: 52 weeks of a subscription ledger — 5 acquisition channels × 5 markets × 3 plans, with gross adds, churn, active subscribers and new-subscriber revenue each week.

Link: ReaderGrowthPulse-SubscriptionAnalytics/ReaderGrowthPulse

Full dashboard with the default 13-week window.

Visuals

KPI Summary

Totals for the chosen window: Gross Adds (new subscribers), Churned, Net Adds (gross − churn), New Sub Revenue, Monthly Churn % (churn per month as a share of the ending active base) and Retention Health (0–100; 100 − 8 × monthly churn, capped).

Net Adds by Market and Plan

A highlight table: rows are plans (Annual, Family, Monthly), columns are markets (BR, DE, IN, UK, US). Darker cells mean more net adds. It shows at a glance which market–plan combinations are growing.

Growth Insight

Auto-written: compares net adds in the current window with the previous window of the same length and says whether growth is accelerating (up more than 5%), slowing (down more than 5%) or steady, then quotes churn and retention health and gives a recommendation. Change the window or any filter and it rewrites.

Channel Mix

Pie of gross adds by acquisition channel (Organic search, Paid social, Referral, Email, App store) for the window. Colours match the Channel legend.

A/B Test Verdict

A two-proportion z-test on the onboarding experiment. Type the control and variant conversion rates and the sample per arm; the text reports the relative lift, the z-score and a verdict — "significant at 95% confidence — ship the variant" when |z| ≥ 1.96, otherwise "keep testing".

Weekly Adds vs Churn

Two stacked panels: weekly gross adds (top) and weekly churn (bottom) across the window. Growth is healthy when the top line rises faster than the bottom one.

Filters and controls

ControlWhat it does
Plan / Channel / Market (checkboxes)Narrow every visual to the ticked plans, channels or markets. Useful questions: "how is Paid social doing in India?" — tick only those two.
Window (weeks) — 4 to 26How many of the most recent weeks the dashboard covers. The insight text compares this window with the previous one of the same length.

3. Security Posture Scorecard — Org and Team Risk

Question it answers: How secure is each organisation and team, and where should leadership push first?

Data: 24 teams in 5 orgs (~2,900 people), scored 0–100 in six control domains aligned to NIST CSF (Identity & access, Patching & vulnerabilities, Data protection, Awareness training, Incident response, Third-party risk), for this quarter and last, with open findings, critical findings and median days to fix.

Link: SecurityPostureScorecard-OrgandTeamRisk/SecurityPostureScorecard

Full dashboard with default domain weights.

Visuals

Composite Score

This Quarter Score and Last Quarter Score: each is the headcount-weighted average of the six domain scores, with each domain counted according to the weight you set. Grades: A ≥ 90, B ≥ 80, C ≥ 70, else D.

Exposure vs Remediation

One bubble per team. Across: average open findings (exposure). Up: median days to fix (how slowly they remediate). Bubble size = headcount; colour = critical findings open (0 / 1–2 / 3+). The worst place to be is top-right and red — many findings, slow to fix, with critical ones open.

Security Insight

Auto-written: composite score and grade, movement since last quarter, number of critical findings open across the teams in scope, and a nudge to raise the weight of the weakest domain to see how much the composite moves.

Score by Domain

Headcount-weighted score for each of the six domains this quarter. The shortest bar is where the organisation is weakest.

Team Leaderboard

All 24 teams ranked by weighted score, coloured by grade (A red, B blue, C orange, D aqua — see the Grade legend). Scroll to see the bottom of the list.

Filters and controls

ControlWhat it does

Raise Patching to 50 and watch the composite, the grades on the leaderboard and the insight text all change — that is the "what matters most" conversation with leadership.

4. Hospital Margin and Quality — Health System Finance

Question it answers: Where does the operating margin come from, and is quality keeping pace with finance?

Data: 4 facilities × 6 service lines × 4 quarters: gross charges, net revenue, contribution, operating expense, bad debt, payer mix, days in A/R and five quality measures with CMS-style benchmarks.

Link: HospitalMarginandQuality-HealthSystemFinance/HospitalMarginandQuality

Full dashboard, all facilities, service lines and quarters.

Visuals

KPI Tiles

Net Revenue ($M), Operating Margin % ((net revenue − operating expense) ÷ net revenue), Days in A/R, Readmission % and HCAHPS Top-Box %. The quality numbers are weighted by net revenue so big service lines count more.

Margin Trend by Facility

Operating margin % by quarter (Q4 FY25 → Q3 FY26), one line per facility. The dip in Q1 FY26 and recovery afterwards is the pattern to talk about; Chenango Memorial has the most volatile margin.

Commentary

Auto-written: net revenue and margin with a verdict against the 4% healthy-system benchmark, how many of the five quality measures meet the benchmarks you set, and the dollar value of shifting payer mix toward commercial by the number of points you choose.

Quality Measures

Revenue-weighted Avg LOS (days), HCAHPS Top-Box %, Infection Index, Mortality % and Readmission %. Compare each with the benchmark parameter of the same name; the commentary counts how many are met.

Service Line Contribution

Contribution margin ($M) by service line, sorted, coloured by operating margin % (orange = low, blue = high). Cardiology carries the system; Emergency and Maternity are thin.

Notes for interviews

Each workbook uses a single extract built from a CSV; every KPI is a calculated field (no hard-coded numbers), every target is a parameter, and the commentary panels are string calculations that branch with IF/ELSEIF on the live values. The same four datasets and formulas exist as standalone HTML dashboards in the GitHub portfolio, so the Tableau and web versions reconcile to the same numbers.

Images on this page are loaded live from Tableau Public, so they always reflect the latest published version.
